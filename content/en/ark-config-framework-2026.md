## 1. The era of "anything you buy goes up" is over

Two decades of property gold, the internet wealth boom, P2P high yields for the blindly trusting — looking back, a lot of people's wealth grew on "era dividends," not "allocation skill." In 2026 we're standing at a turning point: the dividends are fading, volatility is rising, and "buy with your eyes closed" no longer holds.

## 2. A plain framework that actually works

Whatever the size of your assets, a healthy family allocation needs at least four blocks:

1. **Base cash layer:** liquidity covering six months to a year of expenses — local and safe.
2. **Defensive layer:** physical gold and the like, for extreme scenarios, roughly 5%.
3. **Income & growth layer:** long-term, compounding core assets that beat inflation.
4. **Opportunity layer:** equities, alternatives, and the like — take on volatility for higher returns, but keep the proportion strict.

Each block has its job; none should dominate, but none can be missing.

## 3. Where offshore assets fit

Offshore allocation — Hong Kong savings insurance above all — most often lands in block 3, the "income & growth layer," as its foundational ballast: long-term, USD-denominated, compounding. It doesn't chase windfall profits, but it delivers cycle-crossing, certain growth.

As for block 4, the opportunity layer, alternatives like crypto can have a place, but keep the share small (the usual rule is under 3%) — its role is "advance guard," not "home base."

## 4. A framework's value isn't precision — it's discipline

No one can predict next year accurately. The real use of a framework is to give you an anchor that emotion can't knock off course — don't chase rallies, don't panic in selloffs, rebalance by structure.

> The deciding factor in allocation was never "calling the next trend" — it's "having a foundation no matter where the trend goes."
