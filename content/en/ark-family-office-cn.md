## 1. "Family office" became a buzzword

In the past two years, "family office" has been repeated constantly in wealth circles. As if you're not truly rich until you've set one up. We understand the pull of that narrative — but we also want to pour a little cold water on it: under China's current soil, what separates the family-office ideal from reality isn't money. It's the system.

## 2. The ideal: a one-stop wealth hub

The ideal family office is the family's "chief financial officer" — managing investment, tax, succession, governance, even family education. It pulls scattered wealth into one orderly system. The picture is beautiful, and it's genuinely what top families need.

## 3. The reality: three thresholds

- **Legal & tax threshold:** the onshore trust and succession legal framework is still evolving, tax certainty is thin, and many structures can only "go around" the rules.
- **Cost threshold:** a truly professional family office easily runs into millions of RMB a year in operating cost — without enough asset mass behind it, it can't stand.
- **Talent threshold:** a household CFO who understands investing, law, and tax, and is also loyal, is extremely rare.

Stack these three thresholds and many a "family office" degrades into an empty shell with a nameplate, or an expensive consultant.

## 4. The offshore detour you can't avoid

Precisely because of these thresholds, families with real succession needs often turn their eyes offshore — where Hong Kong and Singapore have far more mature legal and financial infrastructure. And the first step into offshore is often not a complex trust, but a single **Hong Kong savings insurance policy**: compliant, transparent, low-threshold — a way to get "offshore" actually running first.

> The family-office dream can be huge, but the first real step is usually very concrete.
