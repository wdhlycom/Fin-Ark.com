## 1. Hong Kong doesn't want a "bigger jewelry market"

Hong Kong is moving toward a **global gold reserve, clearing, and pricing center** — not restarting a jewelry wholesale business. From the policy address and the Financial Services and Treasury Bureau's and HKMA's statements, the keywords are:

- **Reserve hub**: raise gold storage capacity to over 2,000 tonnes within three years
- **Central clearing**: the gold central clearing and settlement system began trial operation in 2026, formal launch Q1 2027
- **RMB pricing**: promote RMB-denominated, physically settled gold futures and develop offshore RMB gold products
- **Exchange Fund accumulation**: the HKMA is studying a modest increase in the Exchange Fund's gold holdings and transferring existing stocks to designated warehouses

In short, Hong Kong isn't competing for Shuibei's business — it's targeting **London, New York, and Singapore's positions in gold reserve, clearing, and offshore pricing.**

## 2. Hong Kong and Shuibei: a division of labor, not a clean break

Shuibei's strengths are processing, wholesale, retail, design, and supply chain — it remains China's largest gold jewelry distribution hub. But over recent years it has shown some problems: "pre-pricing," "gold borrowing," and "deferred settlement" were twisted into high-leverage speculation; material supplier redemption risks emerged in early 2026; and its retail price advantage shrank after the 2025 tax reform.

So the division of labor between the two places is becoming clearer:

- **Shenzhen Shuibei**: refining, processing, jewelry supply chain (industrial and consumer layer)
- **Hong Kong**: clearing, storage, pricing, offshore trading (financial infrastructure layer)

The two cooperate through the Shenzhen-Hong Kong cooperation memorandum, processing trade, and storage connectivity. **Hong Kong will deliberately keep its distance from private leverage and gray-market trading**, because it needs the credibility image of an international reserve center.

## 3. Why Hong Kong suits a reserve center better

It has several things Shuibei lacks:

- **Free port**: duty-free gold import/export, convenient capital flow
- **Common law system**: asset ownership, contract enforcement, and cross-border dispute resolution trusted by international institutions
- **Central clearing system**: reduces counterparty risk in bilateral clearing
- **Airport warehousing and bank custody**: suited for central banks, asset managers, and family offices
- **Offshore RMB advantage**: can issue RMB-denominated gold, filling the offshore RMB safe-haven gap

## 4. On-chain or on-exchange? These are two parallel tracks

A common misconception is "the on-chain token is then securitized onto the exchange." The reality is **one underlying asset, two parallel tracks** — listed shares and tokenized shares issued in parallel.

Take the Huaxia Digital Gold ETF (3418) as an example:

- **Listed class (3418/9418/83418)**: traded on the HKEX, like buying a stock, T+2 settlement
- **Tokenized non-listed class**: issued on Ethereum, subscribed/redeemed through a licensed VATP platform (e.g. OSL)

The Hang Seng Gold ETF works the same way: 3170 is listed on the HKEX, while HSGLD is its tokenized non-listed class distributed through HashKey.

## 5. Buying gold tokens on-chain isn't worth it right now

As of October 2026, the SFC has only opened secondary market trading for **tokenized money market funds**; the tokenized shares of gold ETFs **still have no secondary market** — you can only subscribe and redeem, not buy and sell freely like a stock.

The comparison:

- On-chain fees are higher (about 1.05%–1.2%/year, versus about 0.40%–0.65%/year for listed shares)
- On-chain KYC is stricter (additional VATP KYC + a virtual-asset knowledge test)
- On-chain liquidity is worse (no secondary market)

The on-chain advantages — 7×24 trading, T+0 settlement, programmable composition — are still stuck at the "architecture is ready, waiting for regulators to open up" stage.

## 6. Closing

If you just want gold exposure, buying the listed ETF (e.g. 3418 or 3170) is the more practical choice today. The on-chain channel suits investors already using HashKey or OSL who want to manage crypto and traditional assets on one platform.

The real turning point is the moment **tokenized gold shares are approved for VATP secondary market trading** — only then will the on-chain channel show the practical value of round-the-clock, real-time settlement. Until then, don't mythologize "going on-chain."
