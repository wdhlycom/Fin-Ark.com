> Ordinary people pursue owning more; top tycoons pursue unbundling everything — two completely different wealth cognitions.

Twenty years scraping in finance, I found something interesting: ordinary people seek "owning," top tycoons seek "decoupling."

An ordinary person's wealth is typically highly "coupled": where they live, there their money is deposited, there their business is, all legal and currency risks locked onto the same latitude.

But those who truly passed wealth across cycles share a trait: extremely strong "offshore thinking." They understand that in this turbulent era, body and wealth, identity and assets, must achieve physical and logical double "decoupling."

## 1. 🔑 Mistaking "moving money away" for offshore is the biggest misunderstanding

Many are biased against "offshore," thinking it's for tax evasion, or hiding unspeakable secrets.

But in a professional economist's eyes, the essence of offshore thinking is "regulatory arbitrage" and "risk hedging."

Its core is one line: create wealth within one sovereign jurisdiction, guard wealth within another, safer rule-of-law framework.

At home, with era dividends, diligence, and resources, you accumulate tens or hundreds of millions — that's a beautiful "inland surf." But the wave always falls. Offshore thinking is, before that surfboard shatters, parking a lifeboat in the deep-water zone ahead of time.

## This boat needn't be big, but it must be moored under a different legal jurisdiction.

<center>✦ ✦ ✦</center>

## 2. 🌏 The "decoupling" logic of identity and assets

Why do tycoons, clearly living on the mainland, still open a Hong Kong account, buy a USD policy, even set up an overseas family trust?

Because they deeply know the terror of "single-sovereign risk."

- **Identity is the soft spot**: identity determines the rules one must obey where they are.
- **Assets are the armor**: but assets can have their own "nationality."

When assets achieve offshore-ization, they're no longer just a string of local-currency digits, but transformed into hard currency with international liquidity. This means that even if the environment suffers unexpected shocks — exchange crash, policy sudden change, legal risk — they still hold purchasing power callable anytime, unconstrained by that environment.

## The purpose of decoupling is, in extreme moments, to still hold the confidence to "vote with your feet."

<center>✦ ✦ ✦</center>

## 3. ⚖️ Crossing the "wall" for the information dividend

Twenty years a veteran, I firmly believe one line: the cognition gap is the wealth gap.

One of my daily tasks is, through foreign-media channels, accessing global capital-market dynamics in real time. What I see isn't only the "stability and progress" on Chinese-language internet, but more the Wall Street tightening of liquidity, the ECB's policy shifts, and global capital's scramble for safe-haven assets.

While most are shielded inside the information wall, still hesitating over some wealth product's 5% return, offshore thinkers, leveraging these firsthand intel, already lay out assets with "institutional premium" in Hong Kong and even global markets.

## Offshore thinking is, first, the offshore-ing of information. Only by seeing the real global picture do you understand: putting all eggs in one basket is itself a mediocre adventure.

<center>✦ ✦ ✦</center>

## 4. 🏛️ Hong Kong: the first stop of offshore thinking

For China's high-net-worth, Hong Kong is the most perfect, lowest-threshold proving ground to practice offshore thinking.

- **Logic converter**: in Hong Kong, you needn't cross the huge culture-and-language gap like going to Europe/America. Just complete one asset "border crossing," and instantly access the global financial system.
- **Lowest-cost isolation**: a Hong Kong USD policy is essentially a cross-currency, cross-border, legally-isolated "offshore certificate of deposit." It lets assets, without leaving the holder's control, change into a harder armor.
- **Identity springboard**: through Top Talent, High Talent schemes, you can even achieve a micro-offshore of "identity," gaining multiple boosts in education, taxation, and asset liquidity.

I often tell clients: the storm can't be predicted, but the port can be chosen.

Offshore thinking isn't asking people to abandon here, but letting people, while embracing this place's opportunities, hold a "back road" ready to retreat anytime. This lesson is required for every wealth preserver.
