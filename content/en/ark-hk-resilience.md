## 1. Old money exits, new money steps on stage

What propped up Hong Kong's high property prices in the past was a high-leverage model. On the way up it amplified wealth; on the way down it amplified debt. The traditional property families being forced to deleverage is the elimination of an old model — not a shake in the financial foundation.

At the same time, family offices are growing explosively in Hong Kong. Backed by independent, safe politics and law, high-net-worth assets from both global and Chinese sources are flowing back; Grade-A office rents in Central have begun to stabilize and recover. New capital and high-quality talent connect seamlessly — more than a hundred thousand Mainland professionals have arrived, and consumer giants like JD and Luckin have moved in heavily. The demographic structure and business ecology are being rapidly refreshed.

**Hong Kong has never survived on a single industry. It survives on its ability to "catch new money."**

## 2. The financial base is, in fact, more stable

Look at a few hard numbers. In global financial center rankings, Hong Kong sits firmly in the top three, right behind New York and London; IPO fundraising has climbed back to number one globally; cross-border wealth management scale has surpassed Switzerland.

Offshore RMB clearing volume exceeded 50 trillion in a single month, overtaking the Hong Kong dollar and US dollar combined. As RMB internationalizes, Hong Kong is evolving from a "reservoir" of offshore RMB into an "engine" of global clearing.

**As long as China needs to connect to global capital, Hong Kong's "institutional arbitrage" value is irreplaceable.**

## 3. A century of proof: every leap begins in a storm

Looking back a hundred years, Hong Kong never grew in calm water — every industrial restructuring came with a huge external shock. Every leap began in a storm.

- **1940s–50s**: war and population inflow; absorbed Shanghai capital and evolved into a "manufacturing base"
- **1970s–80s**: rising costs; seized reform and opening-up and evolved into a "re-export trade and financial center"
- **1997/2003**: the Asian financial storm and SARS; opened free travel and evolved into a "consumption and tourism capital"
- **Today**: geopolitical restructuring and old-to-new transition; gathered global family offices and offshore RMB, evolving into a "high-net-worth asset management hub"

History proves it again and again: Hong Kong's resilience lies in its gene of "upgrading through every storm."

## 4. What it means for family assets: is Hong Kong still worth allocating?

Some ask — what does this macro narrative have to do with family assets? A lot. The rule of law and free capital movement are Hong Kong's hardest bottom lines — no exchange controls, a simple tax system, and free capital in and out.

Hong Kong policies are naturally multi-currency, flexibly converting between USD, HKD, and RMB; long-term compounding and inheritance isolation hedge the cyclical risk of a single market. Leaning on the Greater Bay Area and RMB internationalization, allocating assets in Hong Kong lets you share the growth of China's top companies while withstanding the volatility of a single region.

**Understand Hong Kong's underlying logic, and you can do your own asset planning through the storms.**

The tide of the era rises and falls, and the towers of Central keep welcoming new glory. Those who see only the short-term pain often miss the long-term historical dividend; those who see a century of resilience have already laid their plans in the storm. In the end, time will stand on the side of those who believe in Hong Kong's future.
