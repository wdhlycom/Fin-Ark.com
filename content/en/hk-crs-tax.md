## 1. What is CRS, and why does it make people nervous

CRS (Common Reporting Standard) is the global standard for exchanging financial-account information among national tax authorities. Since 2017, mainland China, Hong Kong, and over a hundred other countries and regions have joined. Simply put: **your financial-account information in Hong Kong may be exchanged back to your country of tax residence.**

Many panic at the words "information exchange": does buying Hong Kong insurance expose me? Do I need to pay back taxes? Our conclusion up front: **CRS is an information-transparency mechanism, not a taxation mechanism. That it makes you "visible" doesn't mean it makes you "pay more tax."**

> Compliance isn't about hiding yourself, but about knowing the rules and walking in the sunlight.

## 2. How Hong Kong policies are reported under CRS

Under CRS rules, Hong Kong insurers identify the policyholder's tax-residence status and report the following information to the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department, which then exchanges it to the relevant tax-residence country:

- Holder's name, address, country of tax residence
- Policy account balance or cash value
- Amounts of claims received or withdrawals made within the year

Note: what's reported is **account-level information**, not the transaction-level detail of every movement.

## 3. Reporting required ≠ back taxes required

This is the most critical distinction. CRS solves "information symmetry," not "taxation." Whether you owe tax on a Hong Kong policy depends on:

1. **Your tax-residence status** (a Chinese tax resident must report global income, but has exemptions and treaties)
2. **The nature of the policy** (the appreciation portion of a savings-dividend policy currently has no clear annual-taxing practice on the mainland)
3. **Whether a taxable event occurs** (death benefits are usually exempt; partial withdrawals may be involved)

What truly warrants vigilance is gray-area operations that treat Hong Kong insurance as a "tax-evasion tool" — that's where the risk lies.

## 4. Three principles of compliant purchasing

1. **Declare tax-residence status truthfully**: fill it in honestly at application; don't conceal. Under CRS, concealment only invites trouble.
2. **Use proper channels**: through licensed advisors, legally signed in Hong Kong, ensuring the policy structure is transparent and verifiable.
3. **Consult professional tax advice**: for large asset scales and complex situations, a cross-border tax checkup beats guessing.

## 5. Why we actually recommend "transparency"

Many clients worry CRS will "expose" their assets. But look at it another way — **transparency itself is a form of protection.** A legal, compliant, traceable Hong Kong policy is safer than any "hidden" asset. In an era of ever-greater regulatory transparency, compliant assets are the only ones that can truly pass to the next generation.

> Future wealth security rests not on invisibility, but on compliance.

## 6. In closing

CRS isn't Hong Kong insurance's "Achilles' heel" — it's the push that moves it from "gray zone" to "sunlit zone." Understand the reporting obligations, walk the compliant path, and your offshore assets finally feel solid. Fear CRS, not because you didn't buy right or declare right.
