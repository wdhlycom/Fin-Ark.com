> The foundation of wealth was never diligence, nor vision. It is a force many are unwilling to look straight at — force.

In finance circles, most people talk about K-lines, interest rates, PE multiples.

But after twenty years, I'm more and more convinced: if you can't see through the "logic of violence" behind wealth, the numbers in your hand may just be temporary passers-by deposited under your name.

## What truly defines "belonging" was never effort — it's having someone guard this wealth for you.

## 1. ⚖️ Who defines "who this wealth belongs to"?

First ask a fundamental question: that piece of paper printed with numbers, that string of characters on a screen — on what basis can it be exchanged for mansions, luxury cars, and stability?

Because of credit. And behind credit — what?

Master Nao took it apart thoroughly in "Finance's Foundation Is Force." The essence of finance is value exchange across time and space; and the only premise that makes this exchange work is a powerful, unmovable force — force — that guarantees contract enforcement and the inviolability of private property.

Without that force, or if that force itself becomes unstable first, so-called "private property" is thinner than a cicada's wing before the wheel of history. In late Qing, there was Hu Xueyan; in the Republic, the financial oligarchs — which of them wasn't smart, wasn't hardworking? Yet in an environment where force overrides the rule of law, wealth can dissipate with a single notice, even a single glance.

## For a high-net-worth family, the greatest risk isn't loss — it's the power to be "zeroed out."

<center>✦ ✦ ✦</center>

## 2. 🏛️ The logic of taming "violence" with "the rule of law"

Modern civilization could unleash enormous wealth because we used one thing to cage violence.

That is the rule of law.

The meaning of the rule of law is using a set of procedures that can't be casually changed to seal force beneath the contract. It tells everyone: no matter how great the ruler's power, they cannot, on some afternoon, suddenly declare with an administrative order — that someone's lawful property no longer belongs to them.

This sense of security is the bedrock of all financial activity.

Someone once asked me: why could Wall Street become the global financial center? Why has the City of London endured for a century? Not because the people there are smarter, but because the legal framework there — common law — honed over centuries, has proven it can stand against the caprice of power and protect the individual's assets.

<center>✦ ✦ ✦</center>

## 3. 🌏 Hong Kong: a safe harbor standing on the boundary of force

Clients often ask me one line: "Why does it have to be Hong Kong?"

The answer isn't in the dividends of those policies, nor in the rise and fall of Hong Kong stocks. The answer is in Hong Kong's genetic code of the rule of law.

Why Hong Kong is Hong Kong is that it built a "rule-of-law buffer zone" between China and the globe.

- **The moat of the common-law system:** it's the only common-law center in Asia, emphasizing private rights, procedural justice, and absolute respect for contracts. This logic speaks the same language as the world's mainstream capital markets.
- **Decoupling wealth from geography:** placing part of your assets in Hong Kong is, essentially, a "jurisdictional arbitrage." Moving assets from one jurisdiction to another — one that emphasizes private protection and is more international in its rule-of-law framework.

This, is a kind of "force isolation."

<center>✦ ✦ ✦</center>

## 4. 💡 The fulcrum of antifragility should be the hardest-to-touch place

In the first installment we discussed antifragility. Facing an uncertain age, the best antifragility strategy is: place your assets where the law can least easily touch them.

If a lifetime of wealth is locked in the same rule-of-law logic and the same currency system, the risk is one hundred percent. Once the external environment shakes violently, "protecting wealth" becomes impossible to even speak of.

So my advice to clients has never been the simple "buy," but "architect."

Through a Hong Kong account, a USD policy, even an offshore trust — what you're really doing is building a barbell strategy for wealth: on one end, your domestic career and life; on the other, safe-harbor assets under an extremely stable rule of law, sheltered by an international umbrella.

Wealth isn't merely a resource for survival; it's a passport to freedom. But whether this passport is valid depends on the system that issued it — on whether it plays fair.

Twenty years as an old hand, I've seen too many rises and falls. The truly smart don't wait for the downpour to look for an umbrella. They understood early: in turbulent times, so-called safety is securing yourself a lawful position in the place with the most complete rule of law and the most humble power.

This, is the deepest awakening of this generation of wealth guardians.
