## 1. First, be clear: what this "tokenized gold" actually does

On November 2, 2026, at Hong Kong FinTech Week, a "tokenized gold × tokenized deposits" cross-bank transaction landed, widely reported as "Hong Kong's first." Many people assumed this means ordinary people can now buy gold tokens — **it doesn't.**

The real nature of this event: **an institutional settlement infrastructure test**, not a retail product for individuals.

The parties involved:

- **Gold issuer**: Zhaojin Mining (a Mainland miner, 01818.HK)
- **Custodian**: China Construction Bank (Asia)
- **Receiving bank**: Bank of East Asia
- **Technology**: Ant Group's tokenization platform Anvita TaaS

## 2. Breaking down the mechanism: not "buy gold and hold it yourself"

Here's a key clarification — tokenized gold does **not** mean the user buys the gold and takes it away.

The reality: the gold still sits in a bank vault under custody. The "token" is an **on-chain digital certificate (gToken)** that corresponds to one **400-ounce standard gold bar**.

- One token corresponds 1:1 to one physical gold bar stored in a Hong Kong vault
- Pricing references the Hong Kong gold benchmark price (HAU)
- In essence, it adds an **on-chain record** on top of the existing bank gold account system, making ownership transfer traceable and auditable

So it does **not replace physical gold custody** — it adds a more transparent ledger on top of traditional custody.

## 3. What problem it solves: settlement efficiency

Traditional settlement for large gold trades takes T+N (several working days). After tokenization:

- Settlement time has been cut to **T+5 minutes**, with a target of **T+0 real-time settlement**
- Through the HKMA's Ensemble project, tokenized deposits settle via the HKD Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system
- Smart contracts complete trades automatically when conditions are met, breaking geographic and business-hour limits

In one sentence: **this is a speed upgrade for "delivery versus payment," not an opening of "gold for everyone."**

## 4. The most important risk warning

Your sense of security depends entirely on the **custodian bank's creditworthiness and audit transparency**. You don't directly hold physical gold — and however transparent the on-chain certificate is, it can't eliminate the layer of "off-chain asset authenticity + custodian moral hazard."

This pilot is custodied by licensed banks under regulation, far more standardized than purely on-chain projects, but **the fundamental "trusted intermediary" problem has not disappeared.**

> More importantly: the Shenzhen bureau of the China Securities Regulatory Commission made clear in June 2026 that **RWA tokenization activities are strictly prohibited in the Mainland**. This Hong Kong event is a regulated institutional test, completely different from retail RWA investment products. Do not confuse the two.

## 5. Closing

Hong Kong's tokenized gold is, for now, a case of "the technology works, regulation is catching up, but individuals aren't there yet." Worth following, but don't rush to treat it as a channel you can participate in. The opportunity that actually belongs to you is getting this **clear** first.
