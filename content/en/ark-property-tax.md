## 1. The faith in "bricks" is loosening

Property-tax rumors resurface every few years. Whether it finally lands, and at what pace, one trend is already clear: the era of real estate as "the only reliable asset" is receding. Worse liquidity, rising holding costs, weakening appreciation expectations — three forces stacked together have cracked the faith that "buying property = a sure win."

## 2. Our generation is living through a great migration of asset forms

Look back just twenty years: from the deposit era, to the property era, to today's financial-asset era. Each migration happened because the old container couldn't hold the new needs. Today, many people's heavy "bricks" are becoming a burden that needs rethinking — not because they're worthless, but because they're too heavy, too slow, too concentrated.

## 3. Why a policy is the counterpart to "bricks"

A Hong Kong savings policy has no physical form, but it has a clear contractual one: it names the beneficiary, sets the denomination currency, and defines the succession path. For money meant to pass quietly to the next generation, it's far "cleaner" than a house that's hard to liquidate and easy to dispute.

We're not saying a policy replaces a house — we're saying: on the spectrum of asset forms, it precisely fills the piece property lacks most — **liquidity and certainty**.

## 4. Not "sell the house, buy the policy" — rebalance

Please note: we firmly oppose the extreme move of "dump all property and go all-in on policies." The rational path is to adjust the family portfolio from "all bricks" to a multi-layered structure of "property + financial assets + offshore policies." Each layer carries a different task; none should dominate.

> A shift in asset form isn't fundamentally swapping one container for another — it's admitting that no single asset can forever bear all your risks.
