## 1. A number you might be ignoring

Recently USD-RMB quietly drifted back toward 7.3. Most of us watch house prices, wages, the daily price of vegetables — but rarely seriously ask: of my family's net worth, how much is actually denominated in "pure USD"?

Not the handful of cash you exchanged at the bank, but genuine long-term assets with USD at their base, untouched by a single currency's swings. For most Chinese families, that number is probably close to zero.

## 2. Why "pure USD" deserves attention

Exchange rates were never an abstract numbers game. Money set aside for a child's study abroad, money meant to be spent slowly in retirement — if all of it sits quietly in RMB accounts, its real purchasing power erodes quietly with the rate, and you often don't notice until it's gone.

We're not talking down the RMB, nor urging you to bet on the rate. We only want to flag a plain fact: **a single currency is a single risk exposure.** Your home, your savings, your social security — almost all are bound to the same currency. That's not prudence; it's putting every egg in one basket.

## 3. Most people are stuck at "want to allocate, but don't know how"

The principle is understood; the hard part is execution.

Buy US stocks? Account opening, taxes, time zones — many balk before they even start. Exchange currency? The annual quota is limited, and the cash itself is just "another form of concentrated holding." The real trouble: we want to diversify, but lack a "long-term, legal, no-daily-tracking" USD container.

## 4. The offshore USD policy: an underrated "container"

This is exactly the value of the Hong Kong savings policy that often gets overlooked — at its core it's a **long-term, USD-denominated asset contract**. The premiums you pay each year roll and compound in USD inside the policy. It doesn't solve "should I trade the rate today," but it solves something more fundamental:
