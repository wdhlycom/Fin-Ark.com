## 1. Risk-free yield is disappearing

Time-deposit rates above 1.5% are gone, three-year fixed deposits keep sliding, and even money-market funds — long treated as a "safety cushion" — are hovering at low yields. Our generation grew up taking "steady interest" for granted. That era is quietly exiting.

Every major economy is going through a "risk-free yield drought."

## 2. This isn't a short-term swing — it's a long-term trend

Lengthen your view. Three structural forces, not any single year's policy, are pushing rates down: an aging population with a shrinking workforce, economy-wide debt at high levels, and slowing potential growth.

Japan is the clearest reference: when low rates truly arrive, they tend to last not "a few years" but "decades." We're not predicting disaster — we're flagging a fact that emotion may be obscuring: **the comfort zone of "money in the bank earns interest" is systematically narrowing.**

## 3. When "principal- and interest-protected" no longer protects

We used to "lie back and earn" on fixed deposits and wealth products. Now even the lying-down posture has changed. For ordinary families this means one thing: you can no longer rely on savings alone to outrun inflation — you have to start taking "portfolio allocation" seriously.

But the reality is, the moment people leave deposits, they either rush into stocks and get harvested, or hoard a pile of gold that doesn't move. That middle path — steady, long-term, income-generating — is the one almost nobody walks properly.

## 4. Put "certain compounding" offshore

In the long-term projections of Hong Kong savings insurance, one much-discussed figure is a long-run IRR of roughly 6%–7%. We have to be honest: it is not a deposit. It carries volatility and non-guaranteed components, and must never be read as "principal- and interest-protected."

But its value lies precisely elsewhere — with **long cycle + compounding + USD denomination**, it fills the gap that time deposits are losing. The key: it locks in a growth rhythm that crosses cycles and isn't erased when short-term rates are cut to zero.

> When short-term risk-free yield goes to zero, real safety comes from long-cycle allocation discipline — not from chasing a fleeting high interest rate.
