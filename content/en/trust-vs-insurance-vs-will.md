## 1. Succession isn't just one way

When it comes to "passing money to the next generation," many people have only "write a will" in their head. But in reality, wills, insurance, and trusts are three different keys opening three different locks. Use the wrong lock and even the most expensive key is useless.

> Good succession planning isn't about picking the most expensive tool, but placing the right tool in the right spot.

## 2. The core differences among the three tools

**Will**
- Pros: simple, cheap, covers all asset types
- Cons: goes through probate, slow, public, dispute-prone
- Fits: a basic arrangement where the asset structure is simple and the amount isn't large

**Insurance**
- Pros: automatic distribution, zero maintenance, debt isolation, no probate
- Cons: only covers policy assets, functions have a ceiling
- Fits: the "main force" of succession for the vast majority of families

**Trust**
- Pros: strong control, fine-grained distribution, cross-jurisdictional, holds non-policy assets
- Cons: high threshold, expensive maintenance, complex structure
- Fits: ultra-high-net-worth families with complex structures

## 3. An efficiency comparison table

| Dimension | Will | Insurance | Trust |
|----------|------|-----------|-------|
| Setup cost | Very low | Low | High |
| Maintenance cost | None | None | High |
| Distribution speed | Slow (probate) | Fast (payout) | Medium |
| Privacy | Low (public) | High | High |
| Control precision | Low | Medium | High |
| Asset coverage | All | Policy only | All |

## 4. What a sensible combination looks like

Our advice to most families — **"a will sets the baseline + the policy does the main distribution + the trust handles the exceptions"**:

- Will: a floor arrangement for base assets like property and accounts
- Hong Kong insurance: lock the liquid, dollar-denominated assets you want to pass on directionally, inside a policy
- Trust: once asset level and complexity rise, it takes over the complex parts like equity and cross-border

These three aren't in competition; they're a relay.

## 5. In closing

Don't get swept along by "the trust solves everything" talk, and don't be misled by "a will is enough" either. Each tool has its home turf; using them in combination is the mature approach. For 80% of families, playing the "main card" of Hong Kong insurance well already resolves most succession anxiety.
