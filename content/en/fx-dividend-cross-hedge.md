> In global allocation, the biggest variable often isn't the return rate, but the currency hedging most people never even realize exists.

Someone racks their brains within a single system to earn a 1% interest differential. Then, in a one-way currency swing, gives back a whole year's gains overnight.

I often remind fellow travelers: before eyeing returns, eye the currency first.

## The purchasing power and exchange-rate cycle of the currency itself is the more decisive dimension.

<center>✦ ✦ ✦</center>

💡 **1. Core pain point: the purchasing-power cliff of a single currency**

For a long time, mainland families' assets were 95%+ anchored to the local currency. But against the backdrop of alternating global macro cycles and the local currency entering a low-rate, even rate-cut, channel, single-currency assets face a double squeeze:

- Rate-driven depreciation: domestic risk-free yields, like government bonds and large certificates of deposit, keep falling;
- Exchange-rate spillover: if a single sovereign currency depreciates against the global settlement currency like the dollar, measured in international purchasing power, book wealth quietly shrinks.

The underlying logic of multi-currency allocation isn't gambling on forex, but through jurisdiction and currency hedging, always having a hedge tool in different economic cycles, never staking everything on any single system.

<center>✦ ✦ ✦</center>

## 2. A real-account breakdown: the unique arbitrage model of HKD and USD

For Chinese families, the most natural and lowest-cost springboard for multi-currency allocation is Hong Kong. Hong Kong runs a linked exchange-rate system; since 1983, the HKD has been pegged to the USD, held within the 7.75–7.85 HKD-per-USD band.

This means holding HKD is, to a large extent, equivalent to holding USD assets and purchasing power.

A comparison table shows how it hedges risk and locks in dividends:

- Single local-currency allocation: base return, domestic fixed income or wealth products about 1.5%–2.5%; if the USD strengthens and the local currency depreciates, international purchasing power shrinks directly, no hedge; if the USD weakens and the local currency appreciates, domestic assets gain relatively; ultimately exposed to a single system and exchange-rate risk.
- Cross-currency hedged allocation: base return, offshore high-rated US Treasuries or USD savings about 4.0%–4.7%; if the USD strengthens and the local currency depreciates, offshore USD assets appreciate, hedging the domestic purchasing-power gap; if the USD weakens and the local currency appreciates, offshore book exchange rates micro-adjust, but the underlying USD high-interest income keeps accumulating; ultimately no matter which way the rate moves, one side is always appreciating, and overall purchasing power shows constant stability.

🌏 **3. Advanced wealth-preservation tool: the multi-currency free-switch valve**

Duan Yongping can frequently adjust Apple and Tencent in US stocks, switching USD and HKD assets. But ordinary families lack the energy and expertise. Among Hong Kong's current offshore financial tools, the one best suited to land this model is the multi-currency switch function of modern large savings-dividend policies.

This mechanism is precisely designed:

- Initial investment: buy a policy in USD or HKD;
- Free switch: from the third policy anniversary, once a year you may unconditionally convert to other major currencies, including USD, HKD, RMB, GBP, EUR, CAD, AUD, etc.;
- Legal lock-in: at conversion, the policy's underlying cash value shifts at the then-current exchange rate into the new currency's account, compounding anew.

This is like issuing a visa-free wealth passport. When some economy hits crisis, or some currency enters a death spiral of depreciation, you don't need to endure tedious anti-money-laundering reviews at a bank, don't need to cut losses and cash out — just tap the switch button on your phone, and the entire family fortune migrates with one click into the hardest rules-based currency.

## 4. Landing safety rules: cross-currency operation advice for ordinary families

**No high-leverage forex day-trading.** Retail forex trading is pure gambling. Currency allocation pursues systemic safety of the asset stock, not profiting from volatility.

**Golden ratio, the 3–7 principle.** If the family's future life center remains on the mainland, keep 70% local-currency assets for daily operations and living consumption; simultaneously allocate 30% offshore USD or HKD hard-core constant assets. This 30% is safety redundancy — the bottom card that ensures the whole family's quality of life doesn't slide a class when any black swan hits.

**Earmarked funds, closed-loop chain.** Use multi-currency policies or overseas accounts to deeply bind this hedged asset to children's future education, the family's cross-border medical care, or your own retirement plan. Let currency flow serve real life scenarios.

<center>✦ ✦ ✦</center>

## 5. In an uncertain world, diversify monetary sovereignty

Chen Tianqiao turned RMB into USD, buying American forests. Duan Yongping turned domestic dividends into USD, buying Apple stock. The essence of currency switching is hedging the systemic risk of a single sovereign's credit in a specific cycle.

The highest rationality of wealth is to never put all eggs in the same currency basket. Before the wind and waves arrive, use Hong Kong's compliance window to convert part of your wealth into hard assets that can switch across currencies — the clearest hedge layout a family can make in a low-rate era.
